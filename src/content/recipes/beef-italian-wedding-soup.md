A beef version of Italian wedding soup with a caramelized onion and white wine broth, milk-based meatballs, and garlicky kale served separately in each bowl.

Serves 6 to 8.

## Ingredients

### Beef meatballs

- [ ] 1 kg ground beef, preferably 15 to 20% fat
- [ ] 10 g salt
- [ ] 1 cup breadcrumbs, preferably panko
- [ ] 1/3 cup finely grated Parmesan or pecorino
- [ ] 2 eggs
- [ ] 1/4 cup milk, plus more if needed
- [ ] 1/2 tablespoon dried oregano
- [ ] 1/2 tablespoon dried basil
- [ ] 2 garlic cloves, finely grated
- [ ] 1/4 cup parsley, finely chopped
- [ ] Black pepper, to taste
- [ ] Olive oil, for browning

### Broth and soup

- [ ] 1 large onion, thinly sliced
- [ ] 2 tablespoons olive oil
- [ ] 4 garlic cloves, minced
- [ ] 1 teaspoon dried thyme, or 1 tablespoon fresh thyme
- [ ] 120 ml dry white wine
- [ ] 2 large carrots, sliced into small rounds
- [ ] 2 celery stalks, chopped
- [ ] 2 liters water
- [ ] 6 to 8 teaspoons Better Than Bouillon Roasted Beef Base, or enough for 2 liters according to the label
- [ ] 2 bay leaves
- [ ] 225 g orzo, ditalini, Israeli couscous, or another small pasta
- [ ] Red pepper flakes, optional
- [ ] Black pepper, to taste
- [ ] Salt, only as needed

### Kale and serving

- [ ] 450 to 700 g kale
- [ ] 1 tablespoon olive oil
- [ ] 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced or minced
- [ ] 60 ml water
- [ ] Lemon juice, to taste
- [ ] Salt and black pepper, to taste
- [ ] Parmesan or pecorino, finely grated
- [ ] Parsley, chopped

## Instructions

### Make the meatballs

1. Combine the breadcrumbs and milk and leave them for 5 minutes. Add the beef, salt, cheese, eggs, oregano, basil, garlic, parsley, and black pepper.
2. Mix just until evenly combined. If the mixture feels dry or stiff, mix in another splash of milk. Cook a small piece in a pan or microwave, taste it, and adjust the seasoning.
3. Roll into small meatballs, about 2.5 cm wide and small enough to eat with a spoon. Chill for at least 30 minutes so they firm up.
4. Heat a thin layer of olive oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Brown the meatballs in batches without cooking them through, then set aside.

### Build the broth

1. Heat 2 tablespoons olive oil in a large soup pot over medium heat. Add the onion with a small pinch of salt and cook for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply golden and caramelized.
2. Add the garlic, thyme, and optional red pepper flakes. Cook just until the garlic smells fragrant, about 30 seconds.
3. Add the wine, scrape up anything stuck to the pot, and reduce until almost dry.
4. Add the carrots and celery. Dissolve 6 teaspoons of beef base in the water, then add it to the pot with the bay leaves. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 20 to 30 minutes, until the vegetables are nearly tender.
5. Taste the broth. Add more beef base if it needs depth, but keep it slightly under-seasoned because the meatballs and cheese will add salt.
6. Add the browned meatballs and gently simmer, without boiling, for 10 minutes.
7. Add the pasta and simmer until tender according to its package directions. Check that the center of a meatball has reached 71°C, then remove the bay leaves and adjust the broth with black pepper and salt if needed.

### Cook the kale separately

1. Strip out the tough kale stems and tear the leaves into bite-size pieces.
2. Heat 1 tablespoon olive oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook for about 30 seconds.
3. Add the kale, water, and a pinch of salt. Cover and steam for 3 to 5 minutes, then uncover and toss until tender but still green. Season with black pepper and lemon juice.
4. Put kale in each bowl, ladle the soup over or beside it, and finish with grated cheese and parsley.

## Notes

- This uses the core of the existing meatball recipe: beef, breadcrumbs, Parmesan, eggs, milk, oregano, and basil. Ricotta is omitted because the milk panade gives enough moisture for soup meatballs.
- Better Than Bouillon is salty. Starting below full label strength leaves room for the meatballs and finishing cheese.
- For better leftovers, cook the pasta separately and add it to each bowl so it does not absorb the broth in the fridge.
- The broth method comes from Chef Jean-Pierre; the overall soup structure and flexible small-pasta approach come from Adam Ragusea and Not Another Cooking Show.
