A 5-hour beef-shank pho for about 5 to 6 bowls, built from blanched shanks, charred onion and ginger, toasted warm spices, and a final seasoning pass with fish sauce, MSG, and sugar.

## Ingredients

### Broth

- [ ] 1.5 kg beef shanks
- [ ] 3.5 L water, plus more hot water if needed
- [ ] 1 large onion, halved
- [ ] 80 to 100 g ginger, split lengthwise
- [ ] 3 star anise
- [ ] 1 cinnamon stick
- [ ] 4 cloves
- [ ] 1 tablespoon coriander seeds
- [ ] 1 black cardamom pod, optional
- [ ] 1 to 1 1/2 tablespoons fish sauce, then adjust
- [ ] 1/2 to 1 teaspoon MSG
- [ ] 1 to 2 teaspoons sugar, ideally rock sugar
- [ ] Salt, to taste

### For serving

- [ ] Pho noodles
- [ ] Reserved shank meat
- [ ] Scallions
- [ ] Thinly sliced onion
- [ ] Cilantro
- [ ] Lime, optional
- [ ] Chili, optional
- [ ] Bean sprouts, optional
- [ ] Thai basil, optional

## Instructions

1. **0:00 — Blanch the shanks.** Cover the beef shanks with cold water, bring to a hard boil, and boil for 5 minutes. Dump everything, rinse the shanks thoroughly, and wash the pot. This removes a lot of the scum.
2. **0:15 — Start the real broth.** Put the cleaned shanks in 3.5 L fresh cold water. Bring just to a simmer. Skim anything that rises during the first 20 to 30 minutes. From here onward, keep it at a bare simmer: occasional bubbles, never vigorously boiling.
3. At the same time, char the onion and ginger under the broiler, in a dry pan, or directly over a flame until they have genuinely blackened spots. Add them to the broth.
4. **0:30 to 2:30 — Gentle simmer.** Partially cover and let the broth go. Add a little hot water if evaporation becomes excessive.
5. **2:30 — Check the shank meat.** Once it is properly tender, remove the shanks. Pull the meat off, cover, and refrigerate it. Return all the bones, cartilage, and connective tissue to the pot.
6. **3:00 — Add spices.** Dry-toast the star anise, cinnamon, cloves, coriander seeds, and black cardamom for 1 to 2 minutes, until fragrant. Put them in a tea or spice bag if possible, then add them to the broth.
7. **3:00 to 4:30 — Continue simmering.** Keep the bones in the pot and let the spices infuse for about 90 minutes.
8. **4:30 — Strain.** Remove everything and strain the broth. Skim excess fat if you want, but leave some because it carries a lot of flavor.
9. Taste the strained broth before seasoning. If it seems too weak, boil it uncovered for 15 to 30 minutes to concentrate it, then season. Do not compensate for weak broth with tons of fish sauce or MSG.
10. Season with 1/2 teaspoon MSG, 1 tablespoon fish sauce, and 1 teaspoon sugar. Taste. Add more fish sauce or salt for saltiness, more sugar if it needs rounding out, and another 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon MSG if the broth tastes rich but oddly flat.
11. **4:45 to 5:00 — Assemble.** Cook the pho noodles separately. Put noodles and thinly sliced or shredded reserved shank meat in each bowl. Bring the broth back to a full boil and ladle it over everything. Finish with scallion, onion, and cilantro, plus lime, chili, bean sprouts, and Thai basil if using.
