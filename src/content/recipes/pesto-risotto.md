A simple green, risotto verde-style risotto made with Arborio rice, Pinot Grigio, hot broth, cold butter, aged hard cheese, and green pesto. Think of it less as a rice dish like pilaf and more as a sauce: an emulsion of rice starch, broth, butter, and cheese.

Serves 2, based on 150 g of rice. The video measures the rice from the serving guide on the package and gives the liquids as a ratio to the rice (1/2 cup wine and 3 1/2 cups broth per 1 cup rice); the amounts below scale that ratio, taking 150 g of Arborio as roughly 3/4 cup.

## Ingredients

- [ ] 150 g Arborio rice (Carnaroli also works)
- [ ] Olive oil or pesto-flavored olive oil, for toasting the rice (don't skimp)
- [ ] Dry white wine, such as Pinot Grigio DOC from Friuli: about 90 ml (3/8 cup) for a strong wine flavor, or about 60 ml (1/4 cup) for a light one
- [ ] Broth, about 650 ml (2 2/3 cups), kept hot, plus a little extra in case (the chef uses leftover beef broth; chicken or any other broth works)
- [ ] Green pesto, about 1/2 tablespoon during cooking plus 1 small spoonful at the end, plus extra for serving
- [ ] Cold, firm butter, about 2 tablespoons
- [ ] Aged hard cheese, such as Parmesan or Grana, grated, plus shavings for serving
- [ ] Salt, only if the broth is unsalted

## Instructions

1. Heat the broth and keep it hot but not boiling. Cold broth shocks the rice and stops it from releasing starch. Keep the butter in the fridge until the end.
2. Heat the oil in a wide pan. Add the rice and toast it, stirring, until the outside of each grain turns translucent and the center stays white. It should smell lightly toasted and change color a little without going yellow or brown.
3. Deglaze with the wine and simmer until the alcohol smell is gone. The wine does not need to evaporate completely.
4. When the rice starts to stick to the pan, add hot broth, 2 small ladles or 1 large ladle at a time. Let it absorb, then add more. Keep going patiently for about 15 minutes. The more broth you add and cook off, the more starch the rice releases.
5. Partway through, stir in about 1/2 tablespoon of pesto. It replaces the onion or garlic that is usually added at the start.
6. Once the grains have swollen and are almost cooked, take the pan off the heat. Add the cold butter, the grated cheese, and a little broth. Stir hard to make a smooth emulsion. The risotto should be wavy: it flows back into place when you shake the pan, and moves slowly and thickly when you drag a spoon through it.
7. Stir in a small spoonful of pesto, then taste and adjust.
8. Serve topped with Parmesan shavings and a little more pesto.

## Notes

- Arborio has much more starch than average rice, so buy it vacuum-packed. Starchy rice spoils quickly once exposed. Carnaroli is the other classic risotto rice. The difference between them is more about tradition than practice.
- Watch the salt. The cheese adds salt, and so does the broth if it is leftover soup stock. Using salted broth makes the dish easy to oversalt because the salt concentrates as the broth cooks down. Unsalted broth is better; add salt yourself at the end.
- Risotto should not be runny, but it should not be stiff like pilaf either. If it gets too thick near the end, loosen it with the broth you set aside.
- Any pesto works the same way: green, red, or pesto with ricotta.
- Cooked properly, the grains are firm in the middle and soft outside, with a saucy texture binding them.
